# Verification Report — CBSE Class X "Interact in English: Literature Reader" Unit Files

Prepared alongside 12 unit files. Research was conducted via web search against
multiple independent sources per unit (poem-analysis sites, CBSE study-note
sites, and primary text sources such as Poetry Foundation for the poems).
Where a source was thin or a specific "known trap" detail could not be
independently confirmed, this is flagged below with a VERIFY note rather than
invented. No full lines of poetry and no extended prose passages have been
reproduced anywhere in the unit files; all textual evidence is paraphrased,
with any verbatim fragments kept to ≤6 words (e.g. "king of kings").

---

### 1. Two Gentlemen of Verona (A.J. Cronin) — Confidence: Medium-High
**Sources:** Cross-referenced CBSE/NCERT-aligned study-summary sites for A.J.
Cronin's story (character names Nicola and Jacopo, sister Lucia, wartime
backstory, hospital ending) — consistent across sources found.
**Notes:** No VERIFY flags; details were consistent across every source
checked.

### 2. Mrs Packletide's Tiger (Saki) — Confidence: High
**Sources:** Cross-referenced plot-summary and analysis sites for Saki's
story.
**Correction to brief:** Miss Mebbin's cottage is named **"Les Fauves"**, not
"Les Fleurs" as suggested in the prompt's list of traps — this has been
corrected in the unit file. All other figures (1,000 rupees, Loona Bimberton's
11-mile flight, £680 cottage) were corroborated.

### 3. The Letter (Dhumketu) — Confidence: High
**Sources:** Cross-referenced CBSE study-summary sites.
**Notes:** Confirmed the postmaster is deliberately left unnamed in the text
throughout — reflected accurately in the character file (no invented name
supplied).

### 4. A Shady Plot (Elsie Brown) — Confidence: Medium
**Sources:** General plot-summary sources for this less widely available
story.
**VERIFY:** This is a comparatively obscure text with fewer authoritative
sources online than the others in this set. Core plot points (the Writers'
Inspiration Bureau, the ghosts' strike over Ouija-board misuse, the "Helen"
misunderstanding, Lavinia's departure and reconciliation) were consistent
across sources found, but verbatim dialogue could not be checked against a
primary text. A teacher familiar with the specific textbook printing should
spot-check names and small details before use.

### 5. Patol Babu, Film Star (Satyajit Ray) — Confidence: High

**Notes:** No VERIFY flags.

### 6. Virtually True (Paul Stewart) — Confidence: Medium
**Sources:** Cross-referenced two independent plot-summary sources confirming

**Sources:** Poetry Foundation, Academy of American Poets (primary text),

**Notes:** Confirmed setting (Taormina, Sicily, 1920s), the carob tree,

### 11. The Dear Departed, Part I (Stanley Houghton) — Confidence: Medium-High
**Sources:** Cross-referenced five independent CBSE study-summary sources
(Vedantu, LearnCram, English Summary, Studocu, and an AP Government-published
worksheet), which agreed closely on plot sequence and character list.
**VERIFY:** The precise line/page boundary between "Part I" and "Part II" as
split in your specific textbook printing was not independently confirmed.
This file treats Part I as ending at Abel Merryweather's dramatic
reappearance (before his confrontation with the family and his decision to
remarry and change his will, which are Part II events) — this is the most
commonly cited split point across sources consulted, but should be checked
against your own edition.
**Character list:** all six requested characters (Mrs Slater, Henry Slater,
Victoria Slater, Mrs Jordan, Ben Jordan, Abel Merryweather) are included.

### 12. Julius Caesar — Assassination scene (Shakespeare) — Confidence:
Medium-High
**Sources:** General knowledge of the play, cross-checked via web search
confirming the scene is Act III, Scene I; that Trebonius draws Antony away
before the attack; that Casca strikes first; and that Brutus (overruling
Cassius) grants Antony safety and permission to speak at the funeral.
**VERIFY:** The exact line range excerpted in the "Interact in English"
reader (e.g. whether the printed excerpt begins earlier in Act III, Scene I,
or includes any lead-in from Act II) was not confirmed against the specific
textbook edition. The file covers the core, universally-taught assassination
sequence for this unit. Quotations are limited to short fragments (≤6 words)
per the accuracy rules; no line is reproduced in full.
